# Research: selling the inbox and AI assistant to D2C brands

**Document:** Research notes — competitors, the features buyers care about, and what to add to GrowthxAI's inbox so it can be sold to Shopify and D2C brands
**Researched:** 7 October 2026. Prices, funding and revenue change; recheck before using any figure in a pitch.
**Covers:** all channels in one inbox (email, Instagram, WhatsApp, website), an AI bot that talks to visitors, "where is my order", journeys and shopper timelines.

---

## 0. Summary

| # | Finding |
|---|---|
| 1 | Selling to D2C brands makes sense. Copying a full feature list like Tidio's (about 110 features) does not |
| 2 | You already have most of what matters: one inbox across channels, AI replies grounded in your knowledge base, product recommendations, missed questions, a simulator, takeover by a person |
| 3 | The biggest gap is **order data**: "where is my order", order history beside the conversation, and later order actions. That needs a Shopify connection |
| 4 | Buyers praise four things most: order data inside the conversation, one inbox, easy setup, and AI that answers by itself. They complain most about pricing that rises with every ticket |
| 5 | Indian brands are WhatsApp-first. Journeys (abandoned cart, order updates) are the centre of the Indian tools, and your outreach Sequences engine can run them |
| 6 | Outside Gorgias, Tidio, Klaviyo and Shopify, every competitor here has raised under $30M and earns single-digit millions. The field is not locked up |
| 7 | This is a second product for a different buyer. Prove it with three D2C brands you already serve through the studio before building far |

### 1.2 What to study each one for

| Product | Study it for |
|---|---|
| Gorgias | Order details beside every conversation, and actions from the inbox (refund, edit order, discount) |
| Tidio | Fast setup, and how it ladders prices for small brands |
| Re:amaze, Richpanel | Per-seat pricing against per-ticket pricing; Richpanel's customer timeline |
| Rep AI | Starting the chat when a shopper hesitates or is about to leave; product carousels; add to cart |
| Alhena, Zipchat | Filling the cart from the chat; showing revenue earned per conversation |
| LimeChat, DelightChat, Interakt, Zoko | What Indian D2C brands expect: WhatsApp inbox, broadcasts, cart recovery |
| Shopify Inbox | The free baseline every brand compares you with |
| Siena, Yuma | Charging per resolved conversation |
| Klaviyo | The bundling threat: brands already pay Klaviyo for email |

**If you only try three:** Gorgias for the inbox, Rep AI for the selling bot, LimeChat or DelightChat for the Indian market. Install Shopify Inbox on a test store too, since it is free.

### 1.3 What the money says

- **Most of the field is small.** Outside Gorgias, Tidio, Klaviyo and Shopify, every company here has raised under $30M and earns single-digit millions.
- **Zipchat is the closest model to you.** About $2M ARR in about three years on very little funding.
- **India pays less.** LimeChat's filings show about $2.2M revenue in FY25 after five years. Read that as Indian D2C brands being low-ticket: price for volume there, or sell abroad as well. This is an inference, not a reported fact.
- **Investors still fund the AI-agent layer.** Siena raised $17M on 6 Oct 2026; Zendesk invested in Rep AI in May 2026.

## 2. What buyers talk about most

Ranked from reviews and from what vendors lead with. The review evidence comes from third-party summaries and vendor pages, so read the order as directional.

| # | What people talk about | Evidence | Status for you |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Order details and actions inside the conversation** | The most praised thing about Gorgias: view, refund or cancel a Shopify order from the ticket | **Biggest gap.** See §4 |
| 2 | **One inbox for every channel** | Second most praised for Gorgias; also praised for Tidio | Have |
| 3 | **Easy setup** | Praised for both Gorgias and Tidio | Needs a short "connect store, go live" setup |
| 4 | **AI that answers by itself** | Tidio reviewers say it saves hours of manual replies | Have |
| 5 | **Journeys**: automatic messages for abandoned carts, order and shipping updates, broadcasts | The centre of the Indian WhatsApp tools; BiteSpeed's Shopify listing is titled "WhatsApp Chat & Abandoned Cart" | Worth adding. See §2.2 |
| 6 | **Who visited where**: pages and products viewed, cart contents, past orders and chats on one timeline | Richpanel leads with it; Tidio has a live visitor list and cart preview | Worth a small version. See §2.3 |
| 7 | **Selling before the purchase** | Gorgias's own data: about 1 in 9 inquiries is a pre-purchase question, and half of those arrive outside business hours | Product cards already do this; add revenue tracking |

### 2.1 What they complain about

Pricing: costs that rise with every ticket, and AI locked behind higher plans. Also free-plan limits, limited branding on the widget, and slow vendor support. Simple pricing is an opening.

### 2.2 Journeys

- **You already have the engine.** A journey is your outreach Sequence started by a store event instead of a lead being added.
- **Start with three:** cart abandoned, order shipped, order delivered (ask for a review).
- **It is marketing, not support.** It needs the shopper's opt-in and WhatsApp's approval of each message template.
- **Treat vendor numbers with care.** The quoted 12 to 18% cart recovery on WhatsApp has no source that could be found.

### 2.3 Who visited where

- **Build the timeline beside the conversation, not a live list of all visitors.**
- **The data is cheap to get.** Your chat widget already sits on the store's pages, so it can record pages viewed, products viewed and cart contents. The Shopify connection adds orders.
- **Its value is context.** The person or the AI replying knows what the shopper was looking at.

### 2.4 A number to be careful with

The common claim that "where is my order" is 40% of support tickets has no traceable source. The nearest measured figure is from a Taobao study, where order-related issues after purchase (returns, refunds, exchanges, shipping fees) were 56.5% of support chats.

## 3. Tidio's feature list: what to copy

Tidio's features page lists about 110 features. Copying all of them is the wrong move:

- **You can't win on feature count.** Tidio has had 13 years to build this list and sells it from about $29 a month.
- **About half the list is the old way.** Visual chatbot flows, 40 templates and scroll triggers wired into flowcharts were built before AI could hold a conversation.
- **Much of it is for big support teams.** Departments, ticket priorities, custom fields and smart views serve teams of ten or more. A D2C brand in your reach has one to three people answering.

| Tidio section | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **AI agent** (17 features) | Mostly have | Knowledge-grounded answers, product recommendations, missed questions, simulator, takeover, multiple channels. Missing: order updates, and an "answer rate and time saved" number |
| **Order management** (Shopify) | **Add, in stages** | Order status and order history first. Discounts and cart preview later. Cancel and refund last, with a person approving |
| **Channels** | Have | Website, email, Instagram and WhatsApp are in the specs. Make WhatsApp work end to end, since Indian brands ask for it first |
| **Analytics** | **Add two** | Revenue per conversation and a customer satisfaction rating. Skip the analytics API |
| **Live chat** | Check, add small ones | Saved replies and auto-assignment if missing. Skip typing preview, live visitor list, banning by IP address |
| **Flows** | Skip the builder, keep one idea | Take only the triggers: message a shopper leaving with a full cart or lingering on a product, as a short list of rules |
| **Ticketing** | Skip | Conversations already have assign, snooze, label and resolve |
| **Departments** | Skip | Too few people per brand to route between |
| **Customisation** | Mostly have | Widget colours, position and branding are standard and likely done |
| **Apps** (iOS, Android, desktop) | Skip native apps | The reply alerts plan covers phone notifications through the browser |
| **Integrations** | **Add one** | A Shopify app. Klaviyo later. Webhooks cover the rest |
| **Support services** | Not a feature | That is staffing |

The "check" and "likely done" rows are judged from the specs available at the time; the web-chat spec was not reviewed, so confirm them against the build.

## 4. "Where is my order": the Shopify connection

The catalogue sync reads the store's public product list. Orders are private, so the brand has to connect their store, and the AI needs a tool to look an order up.

### 4.1 Three parts

1. **Connection:** the brand gives you permission to read their orders.
2. **Lookup tool:** a function the AI calls with an order number, returning status and tracking.
3. **Identity check:** proof that the person asking owns the order, so no one sees someone else's.

### 4.2 How a store connects

| When | How | Cost to you |
|---|---|---|
| **Now, first few brands** | The brand creates an app in their own Shopify Dev Dashboard, ticks "read orders", installs it, and gives you the Client ID and secret. You swap those for an access token (client credentials grant) | No Shopify review. The token lasts 24 hours, so the code fetches a new one |
| **Later, many brands** | One public app any brand installs with a click | Shopify reviews the app, plus a separate review for customer data (name, email, phone, address) |

- **The old way is gone.** Since 1 January 2026 a store can no longer create an app with a permanent token from its admin settings.
- **Orders older than 60 days need extra approval.** Plain "read orders" covers the last 60 days, which is enough for "where is my order".
- **You can't convert an app later.** Shopify doesn't let you change how an app is distributed, so the public app will be a second app.

### 4.3 What the tool does

- **Asks for two things:** order number, plus the email or phone used on the order.
- **Checks they match** before saying anything. A wrong match gets the same reply as "not found", then a handover to a person.
- **Returns only what's needed:** shipped or not, courier, tracking number and link, expected and delivered dates. Never the address.
- **Works on every channel** the AI already covers: website chat, voice, Instagram, email.

Shopify only knows what the courier or shipping app wrote back. If that is only a tracking number and link, the AI shares the link and does not guess a status.

### 4.4 How to test it

1. **Create a free development store** from the Dev Dashboard. It comes with sample products and a fake payment method, and it returns customer data without review.
2. **Place test orders in each state:** not shipped, shipped with tracking, half shipped, delivered, cancelled, refunded.
3. **Ask these in the chat widget:**

| You type | Pass when |
|---|---|
| Right order number and right email | Correct status and tracking link |
| Right order number, wrong email | Refuses, same wording as "not found" |
| "Where is my order" with no number | Asks for number and email |
| "mera order kahan hai" | Same behaviour in Hinglish |
| "What address is it going to?" | Declines |
| Half-shipped order | Says which items shipped and which didn't |
| Token expired or Shopify down | Hands to a person, invents nothing |
| A store with no connection | Never offers order lookup |

4. **Run it on a real client store in Review mode,** where the AI drafts and a person approves, before switching to Auto.

### 4.5 Prompt for the build

```
Add a Shopify order-status tool for the AI assistant.
1. Per-workspace Shopify connection: shop domain, Client ID, Client secret in Vault.
   Get tokens with the client credentials grant; they last 24 hours, so cache and refresh.
2. Tool order_status(order_number, email_or_phone): query the Admin GraphQL API,
   verify the email or phone matches the order, return status, items, courier,
   tracking number and URL, estimated and delivered dates. Never return the address.
3. Mismatch and not-found give the same reply, then hand over. Limit to 5 tries per chat.
4. Register it beside the product-search tool for web chat, voice, Instagram and email.
   Offer it only when the workspace has a Shopify connection.
5. Route calls through fetchWithHealth('shopify', ...) so the Health page sees them.
6. Write tests for every row in the test table, against a Shopify development store.
```

## 5. What to build, in order

1. **Shopify connection with order status and order history** (§4).
2. **A D2C mode.** A store workspace doesn't see Sequences and Leads. Short setup: connect store, pick knowledge, AI is live.
3. **Proactive messages.** Three rules: leaving with a cart, 30 seconds on a product page, returning visitor.
4. **Revenue per conversation,** plus the AI's answer rate.
5. **AI answering support emails,** if today it only replies inside outreach sequences.
6. **Saved replies and a satisfaction rating,** if missing.
7. **Discount codes from chat.**
8. **Journeys** on WhatsApp: cart abandoned, shipped, delivered (§2.2).
9. **Shopper timeline** beside the conversation (§2.3).
10. **A public Shopify app listing,** once three brands use it.

---

## 6. Before committing: observe, investigate, test

| Step | Question | Answer so far |
|---|---|---|
| **Observe** | What do we actually know? | The inbox, AI replies, knowledge, product cards and voice are built. Competitors win on order data and setup speed. Most of the field is small |
| **Investigate** | What are we assuming? | That studio clients will pay for software as well as production; that Indian brands will pay enough; that WhatsApp works end to end in the build |
| **Test** | How to check cheaply? | Offer items 1 to 4 to three existing D2C studio clients. Watch whether they connect their store, whether shoppers use order lookup, and whether they would pay. Decide on items 5 to 10 after that |
